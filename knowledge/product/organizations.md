An organization can use its own Starchat to explain public services, membership, published offerings or how to take the next step. Each installation keeps its own knowledge and identity. A company, association or municipality can therefore speak about its own work without borrowing another organization's facts.

For a public service, useful answers include known opening hours, published conditions, documents to prepare and a direct link to the appropriate service. For an association, they might explain membership and activities. For a company, they might clarify what is offered and where to find further information.

Choose a small useful first topic and name who is responsible for keeping its information correct. Include the conditions and exceptions that matter. Test both typical questions and follow-ups before making the collection public. Known facts should be stated directly, not hidden behind a suggestion to read another website.

This is information and orientation. A chat answer does not perform an administrative act, guarantee eligibility, create a booking or submit a form. A connected destination may have its own process. Private staff records, confidential customer information and unpublished decisions do not belong in this public knowledge collection.

If the main need is a shared support inbox, ticket assignment or human case handling, an existing helpdesk may be more appropriate. Starchat can be considered for a focused explanation layer, without assuming that integration has already been built.
